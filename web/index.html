How it works

01

A vessel calls, or goes quiet

A report from the bridge, by radio, sat phone or email, or telemetry that stops making sense: a ship at zero knots, aground, or dark on AIS. Faults move the feed, so the detector has to earn its classification from it.

02

Triage classifies, and names what it does not know

Incident type, severity and confidence, with the basis for it and an explicit list of unknowns. Then the rescue centre responsible for that water, and the hulls that can reach the casualty, ranked.

03

A named human releases each message

One draft per recipient, worded for whoever reads it. Every one waits behind the approval gate, and the audit log records who released it and when.

Why a human approves

A wrong message at sea is a second emergency.

Tasking an unarmed tug into a boarding, or a family hearing about a fire before the company does, helps no one. So every draft is born unapproved, and nothing but a named operator's decision releases it. The company and next-of-kin drafts are never part of a bulk release.

Every decision is written to an append-only audit log with its inputs, the model's output and the approver's name, on the assumption that a marine board of inquiry reads it one day. In this simulation nothing leaves the system at all: release writes the log and marks the message sent.

FAQ

Is this connected to GMDSS or any real distress network?

No. SESN is decision support that sits beside GMDSS and never replaces it. It is not connected to GMDSS, Cospas-Sarsat or any real network, and it transmits nothing. Distress alerting must be made by DSC, EPIRB or Inmarsat.

Are any of these ships real?

No. Around 60,000 synthetic hulls move along real sea lanes, port anchorages and fishing grounds. Every vessel name, MMSI and rescue centre is invented, and every incident is raised on purpose, from the control panel or the bridge terminal.

Why does one incident produce five messages?

Because five parties need five different things. The rescue centre needs position, persons aboard and the classification; a passing ship needs a duty-to-assist request and the hazards; a SAR asset needs the threat picture; the company needs everything; and a family needs a letter that a person writes. One broadcast to all of them is worse than sending nothing.

What does the classifier do when it is not sure?

It says so. Unknown is a first-class answer, and every classification carries the unknowns it declared. A beacon with no voice contact gets no incident type, and it still reaches the rescue centre.

Does it need an API key?

No. With no key configured, triage runs on the deterministic baseline classifier, which stays in place as the floor either way. A free Gemini, Mistral or Groq key puts the LangGraph agent in front of it.
